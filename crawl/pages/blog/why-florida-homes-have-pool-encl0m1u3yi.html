Why Florida Homes Have Pool Enclosures: Benefits, Building Requirements, and Safety Considerations

September 3rd, 2026

If you’ve spent time in Florida neighborhoods, you’ve probably noticed that many backyard pools are surrounded by screened enclosures, often called pool cages. While these structures can help keep out insects and debris, they can also play an important role in helping homeowners meet pool barrier requirements and support a layered approach to pool safety.  

Whether you’re installing a new pool or updating your backyard, understanding why pool enclosures are so common in Florida can help you make informed decisions for your home. Here’s what homeowners should know about pool enclosures, Florida pool barrier requirements, and additional safety considerations.  

Why do Florida Pools Have Cages? 

Screened pool enclosures are a familiar feature throughout Florida. Many homeowners choose them because they can help reduce insects, leaves, pollen, and other debris from entering the pool while allowing for ventilation and outdoor enjoyment. 

Depending on your location, a properly installed pool enclosure or barrier may also help homeowners meet applicable local building code requirements. According to Florida’s residential building code, most residential swimming pools must be protected by an approved safety barrier that meets specific requirements for height, gates, and access. 

Whether you have a screened enclosure or another type of approved barrier, the Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) recommends viewing barriers as one layer of a comprehensive pool safety plan – not a replacement for supervision. 

Florida Pool Barrier Requirements 

Florida law generally requires new residential swimming pools to have an approved safety barrier. Depending on the property, this may include a screened enclosure, safety fence, self-closing gates, or other approved barrier systems that meet applicable building code requirements. 

Requirements may vary depending on the age of the home, local jurisdiction, and the type of barrier installed. Homeowners planning to build or modify a pool should review local requirements and work with qualified professionals to help ensure compliance.  

Benefits of Pool Enclosures 

Helps Keep Insects Out 

Florida’s climate makes mosquitoes and other insects common throughout much of the year. A screened enclosure can help create a more comfortable outdoor space.  

Reduces Debris 

Leaves, pollen, and small branches are less likely to enter the pool, helping reduce routine cleaning.  

Supports Pool Safety 

When designed and installed to meet applicable requirements, approved barriers can help reduce unsupervised access to the pool.  

Creates More Useable Outdoor Space 

Many homeowners use screened enclosures to enjoy outdoor living while reducing direct sun exposure and wind-blown debris.  

Pool Enclosures and Homeowners Insurance 

Swimming pools are an attractive feature for many homes, but they can also increase potential liability exposures. Installing and maintaining appropriate safety features, such as approved barriers, may help reduce certain risks around the property.  

Coverage, underwriting requirements, and eligibility vary by insurer and policy. Homeowners should review their coverage and discuss any questions with their insurance professional.  

Types of Florida Pool Enclosures 

Florida homeowners have several options when choosing a pool enclosure or barrier. The best choice depends on your property’s layout, local building requirements, maintenance preferences, and how you use your outdoor living space.  

Screen Enclosures (Pool Cages) 

A screened pool enclosure, commonly called a pool cage, is one of the most recognizable features of Florida homes. These structures help reduce insects, leaves, pollen, and other debris from entering the pool while allowing fresh air and natural light. Many homeowners also appreciate the added comfort and reduced maintenance they provide.  

Safety Fences 

Removable or permanent safety fences can help limit unsupervised access to the pool. Many designs include self-closing, self-latching gates, and are commonly used as part of a layered pool safety approach.  

Permanent Barriers 

Walls, fences, and other approved permanent barriers may help homeowners meet applicable Florida pool barrier requirements when designed and installed in accordance with local building codes.  

Combination Systems 

Some homeowners choose to combine a screened enclosure with additional safety features, such as self-closing gates, alarms, or safety fencing, to create multiple layers of protection.  

Before installing or modifying a pool enclosure or barrier, homeowners should review applicable local building code requirements and work with qualified professionals to help ensure the installation complies with all regulations.  

Pool Enclosures Are Just One Layer of Protection 

A pool enclosure is an important safety feature, but it should be viewed as one part of a broader pool safety plan. According to the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission’s Pool Safely campaign, homeowners can help reduce risk by combining multiple layers of protection, including: 

  • Installing and maintaining an approved pool barrier or enclosure with self-closing, self-latching gates where required 
  • Providing constant, active adult supervision whenever children or pets are in or around the water 
  • Removing toys and flotation devices from the pool area after use to discourage unsupervised access 
  • Learning CPR and having an emergency plan in place, recognizing that preparation can be valuable during an emergency 

No single safety measure can eliminate the risk of drowning. Combining physical barriers with supervision and emergency preparedness helps create a safer backyard environment.  

Final Thoughts

Screened pool enclosures have become a defining feature of many Florida homes for good reason. Beyond helping reduce insects and debris, they may assist homeowners in meeting applicable pool barrier requirements while supporting a layered approach to backyard safety.  

Whether you’re planning a new pool or updating an existing one, understanding Florida’s pool barrier requirements and maintaining appropriate safety measures that can help protect your home and everyone who enjoys it. Homeowners should review local building codes and consult qualified professionals before installing or modifying a pool enclosure.
