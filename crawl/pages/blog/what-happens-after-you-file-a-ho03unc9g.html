What Happens After You File a Homeowners Insurance Claim? A Step-by-Step Guide

October 12th, 2026

Filing a homeowners insurance claim can feel overwhelming, especially if you’ve never experienced property damage before. Understanding what typically happens during the claims process can help you know what to expect and how to prepare. 

While every claim is unique, most claims follow a similar process – from reporting the loss to documenting damage and communicating with your adjuster. 

This guide explains the general steps homeowners can expect after filing a property insurance claim. 

Step 1: Report the Loss 

The claims process begins when you report damage to your insurance company.  

When reporting a claim, you may be asked to provide information such as: 

  • The date the damage occurred (if known) 
  • A description of what happened  
  • The location of the damage 
  • Photos or videos, if available 

Providing accurate information helps your claim move through the review process more efficiently.  

Step 2: Your Claim is Assigned 

After your claim is reported, it is generally assigned to a claims professional who will review the information provided.  

Your adjuster may contact you to: 

  • Discuss reported damage 
  • Ask follow-up questions 
  • Explain the next steps 
  • Answer questions about the claims process 

Keeping your contact information current can help avoid communication delays.  

Step 3: An Inspection May Be Scheduled 

Depending on the type of loss, an inspection of the property may be needed as part of the claims process.  

During an inspection, the adjuster or another qualified representative may: 

  • Review the reported damage 
  • Document affected areas 
  • Take measurements or photographs 
  • Gather information needed to evaluate the claim 

Not every claim requires the same type of inspection.  

Step 4: Document the Damage 

One of the most helpful things homeowners can do is document the damage before cleanup or repairs begin, when it is safe to do so.  

Helpful documentation may include: 

  • Photographs 
  • Videos 
  • Receipts Repair estimates 
  • Records of emergency services performed 

Keeping organized records may help support the claims review process.  

Step 5: Protect Your Property from Further Damage 

If it is safe to do so, homeowners should take reasonable steps to help prevent additional damage after a loss. Your safety should always come first, and you should never attempt repairs or mitigation activities that could put you at risk.  

If you’re unsure how to safely protect your property or what temporary measures may be appropriate, contact your claims representative for guidance.  

Be sure to document any additional damage that occurs and keep receipts for any emergency measures you take, when applicable.  

Step 6: Before Hiring Restoration or Mitigation Services 

Following property damage, homeowners may be contacted by restoration or mitigation companies offering immediate services.  

Before authorizing repairs or signing agreements, it’s important to understand how those services may relate to your claim.  

Depending on your policy, certain conditions, limitations, or authorization requirements may apply.  

If you have any questions about repairs, mitigation services, or documentation requirements, contact your claims representative before signing contracts whenever possible.  

Step 7: Keep Damaged Property When Possible 

Unless there is a safety concern or you’re otherwise instructed, avoid disposing of damaged items until your insurance company has had an opportunity to inspect them.  

If you’re unsure whether something should be discarded, ask your claims representative before disposal.  

Step 8: Submit Requested Information Promptly 

Your adjuster may request additional information while reviewing your claim.  

This could include: 

  • Photographs 
  • Receipts 
  • Proof of ownership 
  • Repair estimates 
  • Contractor information 
  • Additional documentation related to the reported loss 

Responding promptly may help avoid unnecessary delays during the review process.  

Frequently Asked Questions 

How long does a homeowners insurance claim take? 
Every claim is different. The timeline depends on factors such as the type of damage, the complexity of the claim, inspections, and the information needed to complete the review.  

Will my property need to be inspected? 
Some claims require an inspection, while others may not. The need for an inspection depends on the circumstances of the reported loss.  

Should I make temporary repairs? 
Protecting your property after a loss may be appropriate in some situations, but the right course of action depends on the circumstances. If you have questions about protecting your property or authorizing repairs or services, contact your claims representative for guidance before proceeding whenever possible.  

Should I throw away damaged items? 
When possible, keep damaged property available for inspection until you’ve received guidance from your claims representative, unless the items pose a health or safety risk.  

What documents should I keep? 
Keep photographs, receipts, repair estimates, invoices, and other records related to the damage and any emergency services performed.  

Tips for a Smoother Claims Experience 

While every claim is different, these best practices can help the process go more smoothly: 

  • Report damage as soon as reasonably possible 
  • Take clear photos before cleanup begins, when safe 
  • Protect your property from additional damage 
  • Keep receipts and important documentation 
  • Respond promptly to requests for information 
  • Ask questions if you’re unsure about the next step 

We’re Here to Help 

Experiencing property damage can be stressful, but understanding the claims process can help you feel more prepared. 

From the initial report through inspections and documentation, staying organized and communicating with your claims representative can help support the review process. Every claim is unique, and the specific steps may vary depending on the circumstances of the reported loss.
